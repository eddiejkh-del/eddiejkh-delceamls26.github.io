  Today I had the opportunity to learn from the other groups by listening to the results of their research projects and presentations. Seeing how each team approached a different real-world problem demonstrated the wide range of applications for artificial intelligence. Group 3 presented a project that used AI to determine whether a house was destroyed or remained safe after a natural disaster. Their system analyzed images to classify the condition of buildings, showing how computer vision can assist emergency responders in assessing damage more quickly. I found it interesting to see how AI can reduce the time required to evaluate large disaster-affected areas and support faster decision-making. Their presentation highlighted the practical benefits of machine learning in disaster response and recovery efforts.

   I also learned about Group 4’s project, which focused on using AI for the early prediction of cardiovascular disease. Their research demonstrated how machine learning models can analyze patient health data to identify individuals who may be at risk before serious symptoms develop. This showed me how AI has the potential to improve healthcare by supporting earlier diagnoses and more informed medical decisions. Comparing the two projects made me realize that artificial intelligence is not limited to one field but can solve problems in disaster management, healthcare, and many other industries. Listening to each group’s presentation gave me new ideas about how different machine learning techniques can be applied to real-world challenges. Overall, today’s presentations expanded my understanding of the diverse ways AI is being used to improve safety, healthcare, and decision-making.
